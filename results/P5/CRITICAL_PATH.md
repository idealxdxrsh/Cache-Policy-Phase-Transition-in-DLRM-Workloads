## What the paths are (reading of the RTL)
- Depth 16: starts at a frequency counter and ends at a valid bit, i.e. the EVICT-cycle victim selection: Stage B
  (lines 136-146) runs a serial loop over GROUPS = 16 group minima, each step comparing `eff()` (counter + 1) and
  selecting the smaller; `victim_comb` then decodes the write of `valid_bits[victim_comb] <= 0` (line 204). The path
  shows this as a repeated pattern: 11 nodes with fanout >= 9 at regular intervals, each followed by a MUX2_X1 (10) or
  NOR2_X1 (3) and a chain of AOI22/OAI22 pairs (32 AOI22_X1 + 26 OAI22_X1 = 2.1316 ns of the 4.8791 ns). With depth 16
  and 16 groups, each group has 1 slot, so Stage A does no work and the whole minimum search is this serial chain.
- Depth 256: starts at the registered request address and ends at a frequency counter, i.e. the LOOKUP-cycle hit path:
  `req_addr_reg` feeds the 256 tag comparators (line 83) and also the tag write data (line 211); the path then goes
  through an equality compare (OAI221/NOR4/NAND4/NOR4), an alternating AOI21/OAI21 chain (the `hit_idx` priority loop,
  lines 93-96), and the counter increment written back to `freq_cnt[hit_idx]` (lines 198-199).
  Rows 4-55 are 52 consecutive buffers (51 BUF_X1 + 1 CLKBUF_X1), all 52 with fanout 10: abc's `buffer` step built
  a serial chain for this high-fanout net, not a tree. That chain alone is 3.9331 ns of the 5.9343 ns arrival.

## Why Fmax changes little from depth 16 (203.1 MHz) to depth 256 (167.4 MHz)
- The worst path is different logic at the two depths, and neither grows in proportion to depth.
- At depth 16 the limit is the Stage-B serial compare/select chain, whose length is set by GROUPS (16 at every depth),
  not by CACHE_DEPTH. Its non-buffer logic is 4.3936 ns.
- At depth 256 the logic itself on the worst path is short (non-buffer cells 1.5763 ns): an address compare, a priority
  encoder and an increment. What grows with depth is the fanout of `req_addr_reg` (more comparators and tag registers),
  and here that fanout was buffered as a 52-cell serial chain (3.9331 ns). The 16x larger cache adds 1.0552 ns of
  arrival (5.9343 - 4.8791), most of it on a net-buffering structure, not on longer compare logic.
- Not shown by these reports: the Stage-B delay at depth 256 (it is not the worst path there, and only the worst path
  was reported), and what a layout flow's buffer tree (e.g. OpenROAD `repair_design`) would make of the 52-cell chain.
  No claim is made about either. No RTL logic was changed.
